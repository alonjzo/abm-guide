Start here

The account is the unit. People are a weighting inside it.

This is the one thing integrated marketers get wrong, and everything downstream breaks because of it. Your audience is an Account List. Your budget is spent against accounts. Your results are counted in accounts. You cannot target a person who is not at an account on your list, and you cannot report ROI on a person.

Demandbase suppresses person-level reporting when fewer than five known people are identified at an account. A system that measured people as the unit of value would never round them off.

It suppresses them because they are a supporting detail, not the ledger.

Do not overcorrect

DocumentedPeople-Based Advertising is real and it does exist inside Demandbase Advertising. Known Contacts, job level and job function targeting all work. If you tell your team "Demandbase has no person-level capability," they will open the docs and catch you.

The accurate line: people-based options do not create a person-level audience. They tell the bidder which humans inside your targeted accounts to favour. Same account list, same budget, same account-level scoreboard.

Read it correctly

Lift, honestly

Lift is the metric most likely to be over-claimed in a leadership deck, so it is worth knowing precisely what it is before you quote it.

How it is built

A pre/post baseline on your own targeted accounts

The baseline measures a daily average of page views for each targeted account, usually across the 30 days before launch. It is dynamic, to account for the cumulative nature of page views during a flight.

What it is not

It is not a holdout test

There is no control group, no randomised holdout, no incrementality test in Demandbase Advertising. Anything else running in the period — email, an event, a PR hit, seasonality — lands inside the same number.

Say it this way

TP house standardLift compares targeted accounts to their own prior behavior. Treat it as a health signal, not as proof of incrementality. If you need true incrementality, you build the holdout yourself by splitting the account list. Demandbase will not do it for you.

Reached versus reachable

These two get conflated constantly, and the distinction pre-empts the most common panic question — "why is my reach only 60 percent?"

The ceiling

Reachable

An account with at least 50 biddable impressions over the past month across the brand-safe publisher list. A company's digital footprint determines this. A small mid-market account can be structurally unreachable before you spend a dollar.

The actual

Reached

Targeted accounts that received at least one impression. What you actually bought.

DocumentedLow reach is often inventory, not campaign failure. Check reachability before you conclude the media is underperforming.

The one that costs you

Reporting is a build decision, not a report decision

This is the highest-value thing on the page. If you did not split ad groups by journey stage when you built the campaign, you cannot get stage-level reporting afterwards. No amount of dashboard-fiddling recovers it.

There is no journey-stage filter on the Campaigns Dashboard

No vendor guidance foundStage segmentation is structural. You create it at build time by giving each journey stage its own ad group, then read results on the Creatives tab by Ad Group. A single monolithic ad group is permanently locked out of the stage view.

Predictive scores

Fit

Qualification Score

Machine learning that quantifies what defines an ideal customer beyond basic firmographics. Scores every CRM account plus roughly 20 million accounts in the Demandbase database. This is a gate.

Timing

Pipeline Predict

How likely an account is to become a pipeline opportunity in the near future. This is a sort.

These are two different models, not double confirmation

TP house standardQualification is fit. Pipeline Predict is timing. Marketers stack them as "two green lights means extra confidence" — that is wrong. They are orthogonal axes. Qualification gates, Pipeline Predict sorts.

View them at Analytics › Dashboard, select an account list, scroll to Accounts, click the Pipeline Predict tab. Both can be written back to custom Account fields in Salesforce.

The four funnel areas

Acquisition is the hunt. Know where it actually sits.

Demandbase splits the funnel into four areas, and the mapping is configurable at Settings › Journey › Account Journey Builder › gear › Designate Stages › Advertising Funnel Designations. The boundary that trips people up: Acquisition is middle-of-funnel, not top. Awareness ends at Aware. The dividing line is Engaged.

Single-area campaigns are explicitly permitted

Documented"You do not have to select every Journey Stage. For example, you can target only top-of-funnel stages." Running acquisition as its own funnel area is supported and normal. If you leave a stage out, accounts in that stage are simply not served.

The documented mistake is the barbell, not focus

DocumentedDemandbase names one common failure: "focusing too heavily on either top-funnel (brand awareness) or bottom-funnel (sales outreach)" and losing "high-potential accounts showing real interest but not yet ready for a call."

That lost middle is Acquisition. The published warning is that teams neglect exactly the area we prioritize — so this is an argument for funding the hunt properly, not against it.

The real dependency worth knowing

The people layer inverts — this is the one nobody works out alone

DocumentedAcquisition suppresses your known-contact list. The goal is net-new contacts, so spending against people you already have is waste.

Acceleration restricts to known contacts using the Email Exists filter. The goal is moving a live deal, so you want the people already in the room.

Same platform, opposite logic, because the goals are opposite. Get this backwards and you burn acquisition budget on your own database.

Acceleration — where the weight goes

Campaigns designed to help open opportunities move to won revenue. Accounts already in conversation with sales that still need consistent, high-value touches to overcome doubt or delay. Highest spend band of the three, and the shortest distance to revenue.

Do not add a score gate to acceleration

No vendor guidance foundIt is tempting to require high Pipeline Predict, high intent and Qualified before an account enters acceleration. The playbook contains none of those thresholds — entry is opportunity-state driven, not score driven.

Pipeline Predict 85%+ is an Acquisition threshold. An account with a live open opportunity has already outrun the prediction. Gating on it shrinks your highest-value audience for no documented reason.

Measure it on opportunity progression, pipeline velocity, visits to high-value pages, time on site, and SQL-to-won conversion.

Top of funnel, with intent behind it

Intent is not an overlay — it is the stage definition

DocumentedThe Aware stage is "accounts showing high levels of intent in the last 30 days." Intent is baked in, not bolted on. Then the intent keyword set does something most people miss: it "prioritis[es] showing ads to the people in the account who are the source of the intent signals."

So intent does not just pick the account — it steers delivery to the specific humans generating the signal. Blind top-of-funnel has no such argument. Never silo TOFU unless there is intent underneath it.

One honest caveat: Demandbase recommends blending fit, intent and timing, scoring intent by frequency, volume, recency and diversity. Intent-informed, not intent-gated on a single binary switch.

Build it as one campaign

One campaign, one budget, an ad group per funnel area, bid priority set by funnel depth. Budget then follows accounts as they progress rather than sitting trapped in a silo.

One-way door at publish

You cannot swap the Journey Funnel after a campaign is published. You can still change which stages sit in each funnel area. Confirm you have selected [US Journey] before you publish, not Global.

Creative practice that holds up

  • Refresh creatives every four to five weeks
  • One ad group per funnel area, own creative pool
  • Build familiarity first, then specificity — industry-level message before company-name personalisation
  • Judge creative on account engagement, not CTR

What the platform will not do for you

  • Weight rotation by performance — creatives inside a type rotate evenly, full stop
  • Sequence creative A then B inside an ad group. Sequencing is structural: accounts move stages, so they move creative pools
  • Report accounts lifted per individual creative
  • Accept After Effects or video for personalised ads
The schedule-killer nobody plans for

Personalised banner previews take three to five business days to turn around. Personalisation allows one font and one style, with a 30-character minimum placeholder. HTTPS required. No fourth-party pixels — Rubicon, Marketo, BlueKai, Xaxis.

Mid-market and enterprise never share a campaign

TP house standard — Leslie's rule, and the mechanics back itDemandbase's per-account budget guidance is explicitly tiered by account tier and by ACV. A campaign has one budget pool spread across its account list. Mixing enterprise and mid-market forces a single per-account spend level onto two populations Demandbase says should be funded differently — you either overspend on mid-market or starve enterprise.

Add reachability: reach depends on digital footprint, so a mixed list systematically over-delivers to large accounts and under-delivers to small ones. Your blended reach percentage becomes meaningless. Separate them. Different messaging, different campaigns.

Buying Group Insights — and what to do with it

Found at Analytics › Insights › Buying Group Insights. It shows which personas and buying roles are engaged at each stage, so you can find where to focus and avoid inefficient outreach.

Diagnosis 1

Reached, but group not populated

Ads are landing but the buying group is thin. That is a contact-acquisition problem, not a media problem. Do not spend more.

Diagnosis 2

Populated, but flat

The group is there and not engaging. That is a creative and message problem. Change the creative or spend.

Diagnosis 3

Wrong roles engaging

Practitioners only, no economic buyers. Adjust job level and job function prioritization in Campaign Builder Step 1.

Persona Completeness is not buying-group coverage

TP standard — this one has already cost timePersona Completeness is the percentage of configured personas represented in one buying group by known people in first-party data. It is a title match. It is not evidence of participation, and it is scoped to a single buying group.

At TP, all 10,000 scored accounts run on [US] LOBs only, and the mean is 1.03 engaged people per account. Present it as a gap to close, never as coverage achieved. If a completeness number reaches a leadership deck framed as coverage, expect to spend a week walking it back.

Targeting hygiene

The rules that save you later

Known contacts

Always include them

Campaign Builder Step 1, "Contacts you already know." Requires a Person List with at least 100 records carrying unique email addresses. The bidder then prioritises impressions to matched known people even without an intent signal, and it unlocks the Known Contacts reporting tab.

Non-obvious, high value

One Person List per campaign

The Person List cannot be changed or removed after a campaign is published — but you can change that list's selector criteria any time. A dedicated list per campaign lets you adjust criteria without collateral damage to every other campaign bound to the same list.

Locked at publish

Ad format, account list, intent keywords and known-contact list are all frozen once a campaign is published. The list binding is frozen; the list contents are not. That is the whole reason for one list per campaign.

Activation

Accounts sitting at nothing

We have a large population of target accounts touching neither Aware nor Engaged, including roughly seven thousand mid-market accounts from the Lighthouse migration. They are not going to activate themselves. But run the diagnosis before you run the media.

Check reachability first

Some of that idle set may be structurally unreachable — under 50 biddable impressions a month. Spending against them produces nothing at all.

Check the tag before blaming the media

Engaged requires site visits, and site visits require the Demandbase tag firing. We have a documented landing-page to Salesforce chain break and a Marketo naked-landing-page gap. Accounts can look idle because measurement is broken, not because they are cold.

Segment before you launch

Demandbase recommends targeting accounts by tier, industry, region or size when creating account lists. Split by vertical and by segment, then build. Look at the line of service, ask what the vertical actually buys, and talk to the BDs in their sales meetings — you are the one who will be delivering these results back to them.

Take it to the room

What you pull, what you share

Walking into a sales meeting with a campaign readout. This is the pull list and the order to say it in.

  1. Coverage. Accounts reached against accounts targeted — and name the reachable ceiling so nobody reads a gap as failure.
  2. Movement. Lifted Accounts and New Accounts On Site. Framed as a health signal, not as incrementality.
  3. Influence. Revenue, pipeline, opportunity-to-won and average deal size for reached accounts. If opportunity data is not flowing, say so rather than showing an empty funnel.
  4. Who is in the room. Buying Group Insights by persona and buying role. Where the group is thin, that is a contact gap and it is a named ask, not a complaint.
  5. Creative read. Which ad group is carrying the campaign, by stage. This only exists if you split ad groups at build time.
  6. The next move. One recommendation with the budget or creative change attached. Never present a gap without the fix beside it.
When you get stuck

Go to the Demandbase ops manager. He will guide you, point you at the right surface and tell you what the platform will and will not do. He will not build it for you — and that is the right arrangement, because the person who builds the campaign is the person who has to read it.
