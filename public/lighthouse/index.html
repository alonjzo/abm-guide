In one paragraph

What Lighthouse actually is

Lighthouse is TP’s approved account-research partner for the US target-account universe. They research, qualify, tier and enrich accounts and the people inside them — using ZoomInfo, D&B and Demandbase — and the approved output lands in Salesforce, then flows to Demandbase for targeting.

The headline program has been building and maintaining roughly 7,000 US mid-market accounts, alongside selected enterprise, government, competitor-takeout, PE/VC and event requests. When you pick accounts for a campaign, you are picking from what Lighthouse built.

The capabilities

Account research

Verifies company identity, firmographics and business fit. Is this a real target, or a name on a list?

Tiering

Applies the approved priority framework. Tier is a strategic priority, not a campaign stage.

Deduplication

LOB mapping

Connects accounts and personas to the right TP service motion, so campaigns target the correct offer.

Campaign readiness

Prepares governed output for Salesforce and Demandbase — reconciled, mapped and activatable.

Boundaries

What Lighthouse is not

Not an unlimited list-building service. Requests go through intake and prioritization.

Not the owner of campaign strategy, Marketo execution, or Demandbase campaign config. They supply the intelligence; we run the campaigns.

Not the final owner of Salesforce data governance. Salesforce is the system of record; Sales Ops and Marketing Ops own reconciliation.

Not a reason to bypass Salesforce. A spreadsheet is a working file, never the source of truth.

One word to be careful with

“KDM” means two different things depending on who is saying it. Sales uses it for a specific role on a live deal. Lighthouse uses it for researched key persona titles — the people we think matter at an account.

Same word, very different lists, and they will not match. If someone asks you for KDMs, ask which one they mean.

Tier means priority, not progress. An account in Tier 1 is strategically important. It says nothing about where that account sits in the buying journey — that is Journey Stage, and they are different fields answering different questions.

Scoring

How an account earns its tier

Every account is scored on the same five things, then lands in a tier. The tier is what tells you how hard to go after it.

Size

Revenue, and whether the business is big enough to support the kind of engagement we sell. This carries the most weight.

Growth

Is the company expanding, investing, or changing in a way that creates demand for what we do?

Scale

Employee count and operating footprint. A proxy for how much work there is to outsource.

Outsourcing propensity

Do they outsource now — not just that they did once. This is the one people underweight.

TP relationship

Whether we already work with them, have won before, or have never engaged at all.

Then: a tier

The score maps to Tier 1A, 1, 2 or 3. Higher tier, more investment, tighter motion.

Big company does not mean good target. Revenue and headcount can make an account look better than it is when they have no real appetite to outsource. That is why propensity carries real weight — and why a BD who knows the account can challenge a score.

A challenge needs evidence and a decision gets logged. The model stays consistent; field knowledge makes it accurate.

Public sector

Government runs on a different model

Same tiers, same output, completely different inputs. You do not need to know the mechanics — you need to know it exists and why.

Revenue is not the measure.

An agency has a budget, not revenue. What matters is the program spend and contract value we could actually win — not the size of the whole department.

Timing runs on procurement, not quarters.

Work comes up for bid on a schedule. Contract renewals, RFPs and funding cycles decide when an agency is reachable, not how much they like us.

Winning can require a partner.

Some contracts are reserved or need a vehicle we do not hold. That can mean teaming or subcontracting rather than bidding direct. It is a route question, not a bad account.

Structure is layered.

A state, then a department, then a program. “State of Washington” is not an account you can target — the agency underneath it is.

Both models live in the same US master tiering list. Commercial and government accounts sit side by side with the same tier labels, so you do not need to track two systems. The scoring behind them is just different.

If you are working public sector, talk to Josh — that model was built with TP Government and there is context that does not fit on a page.

Before you submit anything

Check the universe first

Most rejected and delayed requests are accounts we already hold. The universe is 10,972 addressable US accounts — look there before asking for research on names that are probably already tiered.

Intake

A request is ready when it has all of this

  • Business owner — a name, not a team
  • Campaign or business objective — what this is for
  • Requested account universe — and confirmation you checked the existing one
  • Vertical and LOB — which service motion
  • Geography and target tiers
  • Requested buyer groups or personas
  • Required delivery date — a real one
  • Salesforce campaign or activation destination, where applicable
