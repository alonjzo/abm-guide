Guide · Optimize mid-flight

Wait the window. Then make one change.

Most mid-flight damage is done in week one, reacting to noise. Every signal below has a window attached — the time it takes before the number means anything. Read the window before you touch the campaign.

Pivot thresholds from VIP training, Dec 2025; build session 4 Mar 2026

The rule

No change before its window closes

A campaign in week one is not underperforming — it is warming up. Delivery faults are worth catching immediately; performance is not readable yet. Each signal below fires only after its window, and each has one move attached. Change one thing, then wait the window again.

01

Identify the signal

Which number missed, not which number looks low.

02

Check the window

Has enough time passed for it to mean anything?

03

Make one move

One variable. Two changes and you learn nothing.

04

Re-wait

The window resets. Resist the second change.

The table

When to change something

Five signals, five windows, five moves. If a number is off but its window has not closed, the correct action is to do nothing and note the date.

Add, do not swap. When CTR is soft, new creatives go into the existing ad group and the weakest get archived. Tearing down an ad group restarts its learning and throws away the delivery history you have paid for.

Stopping early is a real move, not a failure. A flight with declining engagement in month two will not recover by running to its end date — it will spend the rest of the budget proving the point. Pull it and redeploy.

Cadence

How often to look, and what you are looking for

What you check changes as the flight matures. Early on you are checking that it is delivering. Only later are you checking that it is working. Confusing the two is what produces week-one panic.

WK 1–2

Daily — delivery faults, not performance

Is it serving? Are creatives approved? Is spend pacing? Performance numbers in this window are noise — do not read them as signal and do not change anything based on them.

WK 3–4

Twice weekly — first real signal

Reach is now readable. The 2-week reach threshold and the 3-week CTR threshold both land here. This is the first window where a change is justified.

MO 2+

Weekly, with a monthly deep dive

Journey progression and engagement trend become readable. This is where the stop-early decision gets made if engagement is heading down.

AUTO

Weekly watcher mail covers the exec layer

Leadership visibility is already handled. You do not need to build a separate status update for it — check the watcher output before writing anything by hand.

Locked at launch

What you cannot change once it is running

Some levers close the moment you publish. Knowing which ones means you spend the build step on the things that cannot be fixed later — and stop hunting for a mid-flight fix that does not exist.

The contact list

Contact lists cannot be added to a running campaign. If person coverage was thin at build, it stays thin for the whole flight. This is the single most common "can we just add..." request and the answer is no.

Fix at — build your account list

Creative size coverage

Reach is largely decided by how many display sizes you uploaded. Five or more is the standard. You can add sizes mid-flight, but the reach you lost in weeks one and two does not come back.

Fix at — Step 0, Inventory Planner

Geo and environment

Set at Campaign Basics. Geo in particular is where teams get burned — verify it before publish, because a mis-scoped geo reads as a performance problem for weeks before anyone finds the real cause.

Fix at — Campaign Basics

Intent signal availability

Not yours to fix at all. Tenant keyword volume is over the 2M/week platform cap, so some accounts are missing intent signal — suppressing dynamic list membership and dampening performance. Escalated to engineering by Kate.

Platform constraint — note it, do not chase it

Before you conclude a campaign is underperforming, rule out the locked levers. A flight at 60% reach with four creative sizes and a mis-scoped geo is not a creative problem, and no amount of mid-flight optimization will rescue it. That is a rebuild at the next flight, not a fix at this one.
