Build a US Journey campaign in the right order

The order is the method. Reach before build, journey before creative, creative before budget. Skip a step and you find out eight weeks later when the spend is gone and the funnel never moved.

Audience: ABM, Integrated Marketing, Vertical Leads Format: Display · Native · Video · CTV Reviewed September 2026
Non-negotiable

Every US campaign runs on US Journey and US-prefixed assets. The Demandbase default journey reads global, all-region logic, and journey selection cannot be undone after publish — pick the wrong one and you rebuild the campaign from scratch.

Step00

Inventory Planner — before you open the builder

Advertising → Inventory Planner. This is a forecast tool, not a setup step. You are answering one question: is there enough inventory to make this campaign worth running?

Feed it the same inputs you are about to use in the builder — flight dates, account list, CPM tier, geo, ad type, and every ad size you intend to upload — then hit Calculate available inventory. It returns potential reach, available impressions, and maximum supportable budget, plus a CSV.

Source: Demandbase Help Center, Forecast a Campaign with the Inventory Planner — the stated purpose is to "prevent setting up campaigns that underdeliver due to a lack of impressions, avoiding unmet goals and dissatisfaction."

The reach killer nobody expects

The Enterprise BFS campaign launched 14 Mar 2026 against 47 accounts and reached only 25 — 53%. Engagement was strong (51% account lift, above benchmark), but half the list never saw an ad. Diagnosis: one creative size uploaded instead of five. One size only fits one placement shape, so most of the available inventory was ineligible. Size count is a reach lever, not a design preference.

Source: TP internal — "db – kate general training," 30 Apr 2026: "Low reach likely due to using only 1 creative size instead of 5 – additional sizes should improve reach by fitting different ad placements."

Supported display sizes — upload five or more

300×250 · 728×90 · 160×600 · 300×600 · 970×250 · 320×50 · 300×50

These are the only seven. Nothing outside this list can be uploaded — the question is only which of the seven you build. If you enable In-App inventory in Step 1, note that in-app placements accept 300×250 and 320×50 only, so those two have to be inside your set whatever its size. Step01

Campaign Basics — who, where, and which humans

Six of the fields on this screen lock the moment you publish. Get them wrong and the fix is a new campaign, not an edit.

Naming and hygiene

  • Name it after the Salesforce campaign code. Attribution is a string match — start with the SFDC code, then the campaign, or the results have nowhere to land.
  • [US] prefix on everything. Advertising runs out of the Global workspace, where every region's assets sit side by side. The prefix is what stops a January-style cross-region overwrite.
  • Labels: vertical, LOB, funnel stage. That is how you filter reporting later without exporting.
  • Watchers: the EVP and the vertical owner. They get the weekly performance mail automatically — that is free executive visibility.
  • Flight: one quarter is the standard. Only the end date can be changed after publish.

Ad format — in plain English

Picked once per campaign. It decides what you have to produce, where the ad physically shows up, whether In-App is available, and in one case what you are allowed to optimize for. Here is what each one actually is.

Display  ·  in use

What it is: a banner. A rectangle or strip sitting around the edges of a web page — top, side, or between paragraphs. The workhorse of the internet.

Where it shows up
Business and trade publisher sites, and — if you tick In-App — inside mobile and desktop apps.
What you have to make
Static images at seven fixed sizes. A designer can turn one concept into all seven in an afternoon. No copywriting beyond a headline on the image itself.
Use it when
Almost always. It is the default because it is cheap, it scales, and it builds the frequency that moves accounts from Target Account into Aware. Every campaign should have a Display layer unless there is a reason not to.

Native  ·  in use

What it is: an ad dressed as content. A headline, a thumbnail and a source line, sitting in the page's own recommendation feed — the "you might also like" row under an article.

Where it shows up
Inside publisher content feeds, styled to match the surrounding page. Web only — In-App does not apply.
What you have to make
One image at 1200×627, a 128×128 logo, and tight copy: headline 25 characters, body 90, CTA 15. The copy is the hard part — 25 characters is about four words.
Use it when
The click you want is "I want to read that," not "I want to buy that." Reports, guides, benchmark data, thought leadership. It earns attention that a banner cannot, and it is the right vehicle for a gated asset.

Video  ·  not yet

What it is: a video that plays in a player — either before someone's chosen video starts (pre-roll) or auto-playing inside an article body (outstream).

Where it shows up
Publisher video inventory. Whether that includes YouTube is the open question — see the note below. Web only.
What you have to make
A finished video: MP4 or a VAST tag, 30 seconds maximum with 15–30 recommended, 16:9 or 4:3, under 200 MB. This is a production job, not a design job.
Use it when
The story genuinely needs motion — a demo, a customer telling it in their own words, something a banner physically cannot say. Not as a general-purpose upgrade to Display.

CTV  ·  not yet

What it is: Connected TV. A spot on a streaming app running on an actual television — the ad break inside a streaming service on the living-room screen.

Where it shows up
Streaming apps on TV devices. Web only in the sense that In-App does not apply.
What you have to make
VAST 2.0+ or MP4, exactly 15 or 30 seconds — not 14, not 32 — at 1280×720 or 1920×1080, 15–30 Mbps CBR, 48 kHz audio. The strictest spec of the four.
Use it when
Presence is the point. There is no click on a TV screen, so CTV cannot be optimized for clicks at all — it changes what the Step 2 outcome table can do. Reserve it for named-account programs where "we saw your ad" carries weight in the room.

Source: Demandbase Help Center, Non-Personalized Ad Specifications (all four creative specs, quoted) and Campaign Builder Step 1 (In-App is Display-only; CTV restricts Clicks optimization). Usage: live Advertising dashboard, Tenant 15053 — 11 Display and 3 Native campaigns active 11 Aug – 11 Sep 2026, no Video, no CTV.

Open question before anyone plans a video buy

The current help-center spec for Video describes MP4 and VAST tags at standard web dimensions, which reads as open-web publisher inventory. Demandbase's own announcement of B2B video advertising, however, names YouTube as the platform. Those two do not reconcile from public documentation, and it matters — a YouTube buy and an open-exchange video buy are different media plans with different costs.

Confirm with Maria before scoping anything. Separately, there is a second and definitely-available route to YouTube: our Google integration is connected with Orchestration and Campaign Reporting enabled, and an Orchestration Audience with a Google destination already exists. Pushing a Demandbase audience into Google Ads and running YouTube there is a smaller step than standing up a Demandbase video buy.

Source: Demandbase Help Center, Non-Personalized Ad Specifications (VAST/MP4 web video specs); Demandbase press release, Demandbase Introduces Video Advertising for B2B (names YouTube); TP internal — US Workspace audit, Section 12: Google connected, 2 accounts, Orchestration and Campaign Reporting enabled.

Should we add a format? Not yet — and here is the order

First, fix fill on the format we already run. Reached is 71% portfolio-wide. Adding a new format before solving that is spending new money on a problem we have not fixed — and the fix is free: more sizes.

Second, settle Display versus Native properly. The three Native campaigns averaged roughly 0.10% CTR against roughly 0.05% across the eleven Display campaigns, and the best performer in the portfolio is Native at 0.209%. But two of those three sit mid-pack and three campaigns is not a finding. Run it as a controlled test — same list, same message, same flight — and let it decide where the next dollar goes.

Then look at video, once the inventory question is answered and we have assets on spec. The honest read on CTV: it is a brand-presence format with no click path, so it will not show up in the funnel metrics leadership currently reads. That is a positioning conversation with Leslie before it is a media buy.

Environment — Web and In-App

Two checkboxes, and only one of them is a decision. Web is always on and cannot be turned off — "Serves ads on websites. This option is always included." In-App is the optional one: "Serves ads in mobile, tablet, and desktop apps," and it is "included at no additional cost."

The trap — a fill failure, not a targeting failure

These are not new sizes. 300×250 and 320×50 are already two of the seven display sizes; In-App simply accepts only those two. So the requirement is not "add two more," it is "your set must contain these two." Build five sizes that happen to be 728×90, 160×600, 300×600, 970×250 and 300×50 and you have full web coverage and zero in-app coverage, with the box ticked.

Nothing errors, because nothing is wrong. Valid creative, valid targeting, valid budget — there is simply no dimension match, so every in-app request comes back with nothing to serve and the impressions quietly route to web. Your reach number lands exactly where it would have with In-App switched off. Same shape as the Enterprise BFS campaign: the accounts were reachable, the creative was not servable.

When to turn it on

On for awareness and any campaign that came out of the Inventory Planner short on reach — it is free incremental supply and it is the cheapest lever you have. Consider off for a 1:1 named-account play where placement quality is part of the message: MAID-matched app inventory can put an executive impression inside a consumer app, which counts as reach but carries less weight than a trade-press placement. That second call is a judgment on our side, not a platform rule — the platform is neutral on it.

Geo — where teams get burned

Never click the North America preset

The regional preset includes Mexico, which belongs to LATAM. Open the region node and tick United States of America and Canada individually, leaving Mexico unchecked. The parent checkbox should sit in the half-filled state, not solid. Solid means you just bought LATAM impressions on a US budget.

Source: TP internal — US Workspace platform rules, and the 15 Apr 2026 lists walkthrough: "Set regional targeting to North America (exclude Mexico as it belongs to Latin America)."

A buying group is not a list of people. It is a set of personas — role families — that Demandbase resolves into real humans at your target accounts. You supply the companies; the buying group tells the platform which chairs to aim at inside them. You may attach more than one, and the US tenant has exactly three live.

How to pick

CX motion → CX Core alone. Solution or LOB motion (TAS, F&A, Data, RaaS) → LOBs, plus Vertical Specialists if the creative is industry-specific. Contacts you already know

Optional person list. Leave it empty on a one-to-many campaign — Demandbase will find the people at your accounts on its own. Attach it only on a true 1:1 or 1:few play where you have named the committee. And check whose list it is: a named-account contact list left over from a previous build is the single most common copy-paste error in the builder, and it cannot be swapped after publish.

Job level / function

Two modes: Prioritize (bid more on these people) or Exclude, up to five combinations. Prioritize is the default posture. Standard awareness stack: C-Level, Senior Executive, Vice President, Director — Manager excluded at the top of funnel. Then maturate as the account moves: Director-heavy in Aware, VP/SVP/EVP in Engaged and Pipeline.

Step02

Journey Targeting — how the US Journey actually works

Marked OPTIONAL in the interface. Treat it as mandatory. This screen is where a flat ad buy becomes an account-based program.

The US Journey holds seven stages: All Other, Target Account, Aware, Engaged, SQA, MQA, Opportunity, Customer. The builder shows them as chips in Available Journey Stages with a live account count on each. You drag those chips into four funnel buckets — Aware, Engaged, Pipeline, Customer — and each bucket gets its own campaign outcome, its own optimization target, and its own ad groups.

That is the whole mechanism: the funnel bucket is the unit of strategy, the journey stage is the unit of population, and the ad group is the unit of message. One campaign, several conversations, running at once.

Select all three working funnels, not just one

Accounts move. When engagement points push an account from Aware to Engaged, Demandbase automatically shifts it into the Engaged funnel and starts serving that funnel's creative — but only if you built that funnel. An Aware-only campaign watches accounts progress and then has nothing to say to them.

Aware-only is correct for one case: net-new market entry with zero existing engagement.

Source: TP internal — 4 Mar 2026 campaign build: "For future campaigns: Select all three journey stages to enable automatic optimization… Awareness stage accounts moved to appropriate journey stages based on engagement points automatically."

A funnel bucket holding 0 accounts will accept ad groups, consume your build time, pass validation, and then never serve a single impression. If Engaged reads 0, either leave that funnel empty or fix the upstream engagement threshold first. Counts move daily — SQA re-evaluates by 6 AM PST — so never publish a fixed count to a stakeholder.

Step03

Ad Groups — two sets per funnel, four creatives each

This is the step the team keeps getting wrong, and it is the step that decides whether the campaign compounds or plateaus.

The failure mode

Build one set of five creatives and drop the same set into every funnel. It passes validation. It looks finished. It then does three things: it says the same thing to a cold account and a live opportunity, it makes creative performance unreadable because every stage reports identical assets, and it burns frequency on accounts that already converted past that message. You bought an account-based platform and ran a flat banner buy through it.

The standard: 2 × 4 per funnel

Every funnel you built in Step 2 gets two ad groups — one Targeting, one Retargeting — with four creatives in each. Three working funnels means six ad groups and roughly 24 creatives, sized across the five-plus display dimensions from Step 0.

Aware
Targeting · 4

Category and problem framing. Market pressure, industry data, the pain in their language. No product.

+ Retargeting · 4

Same theme, second exposure — the proof point or stat that follows the first ad.

Engaged
Targeting · 4

Solution overview, technical guide, case study. Soft gate. This is where the offer appears.

+ Retargeting · 4

For people who visited but didn't convert — the objection-handler or the second asset.

Pipeline
Targeting · 4

Demo, discovery, ROI, implementation proof. Named-account specificity where the deal warrants it.

+ Retargeting · 4

Close-the-gap creative for the committee members who are already on your site.

How retargeting actually works here

Retargeting in Demandbase is not a separate campaign. It is a targeting type set on the ad group, with three options:

Targeting typeWho sees itUse it for
AllAnyone matching Standard or retargeting definitionsThe default. Fine for a single-ad-group campaign, useless for optimization.
StandardFirst-time site visitors onlyYour Targeting set. Net-new exposure.
User retargetingOnly people who previously hit the site and carry the retargeting pixelYour Retargeting set. Second touch on a known warm person.

Source: Demandbase Help Center, Campaign Builder Step 3: Add Ad Groups — "Standard: shows creatives only to first-time website visitors"; "User retargeting: displays creatives exclusively to previous website visitors with a retargeting pixel"; multiple creatives within a type "rotate evenly."

So why does "All" exist at all?

Fair question — Standard and User retargeting are mutually exclusive and between them they cover everybody, so All looks redundant. It is the union of the other two, and it exists for three reasons that are worth knowing before you dismiss it.

  • It is the only default that cannot starve. If the platform defaulted to Standard, every campaign built by someone who never touched the field would silently never reach a returning visitor. If it defaulted to User retargeting, a brand-new campaign would have nobody to serve at all. All is the safe default precisely because it makes no assumption — which is also why it is the wrong choice once you know what you are doing.
  • It is correct for a single-ad-group campaign. One creative set, one message, no intention of reading the two cohorts separately — then splitting the audience buys you nothing and All is the right pick. It also satisfies the platform rule below on its own.
  • It absorbs the cold-start ramp. On day one the retargeting pool is empty — nobody has visited yet from this campaign. A User retargeting ad group ramps only as the Standard set drives traffic. On a short flight that pool may never get large enough to read.
The decision rule

The cost of All is not delivery — it delivers fine. The cost is measurement: one blended CTR instead of two readable ones, and no way to tell whether a message works cold or only on someone who already knows you.

So: split into Standard + User retargeting on a quarter-long flight, which is our standard and gives the retargeting pool time to build into something worth reading. Use All on a short burst — a four-to-six week event push, a single-message test — where the pool will never mature and the split would just produce two thin numbers. That call is ours, not a platform rule.

Before you trust the Retargeting half, check the pixel

User retargeting only matches people carrying the retargeting pixel, so its audience is capped by site-tag coverage, not by your budget. If a Retargeting ad group under-delivers, check tag coverage before you blame the creative — the pool may simply be small. Current signal is good: the live flight shows 1,276 accounts visited and 306 new accounts on site, so site-side tracking is returning data.

Hard platform rule

At least one ad group in the campaign must use Standard or All. A campaign built entirely on User retargeting has no way to acquire a first visitor, so there is nobody to retarget and delivery stalls. Targeting feeds Retargeting — that is the dependency, and it is why the two sets ship together.

Why this is "retargeting for optimization"

Creatives inside an ad group rotate evenly. The platform does not pick a winner for you across mixed audiences — it picks within a defined one. Splitting Standard from User retargeting gives you two clean cohorts running the same four-creative rotation, so the click-through read is real: which message works on a cold account versus which message works on someone who already came to the site. That is the A/B you can actually act on. One blended ad group averages the two into a number that tells you nothing.

Then you optimize: rename creatives so they are identifiable in reporting, track CTR by asset, retire the bottom performers, and rotate the set.

Source: Demandbase Help Center, The Creative Library — ad groups exist to manage "different shapes and sizes of a creative theme and perform A/B testing by running different groups simultaneously." TP internal, 4 Mar 2026: "Track click-through rates by creative asset to identify top performers. Rename creative assets for easier identification."

Creative Library — build before, patch in flight

Advertising → Creative Library. The answer to "do I load creative before launch or after?" is before, and the reason is mechanical, not stylistic.

Action on a live campaignWhat happens
Add a creative into an existing ad groupSafe. Campaign keeps running.
Archive one creativeSafe. Pauses that creative only; the rest keeps serving.
Bulk-update the landing page on an ad groupSafe. Applies to every creative in that group at once.
Move a creative between ad groupsPauses the campaign. You must go back into Campaign Builder and re-select the ad group to restart it.
Move an ungrouped creative into an ad groupRemoves it from the campaigns it was attached to.

Source: Demandbase Help Center, Understanding the Creative Library, Ad Groups, and Ungrouped Creatives.

The operating rule

Build and name the ad groups in the Creative Library before you reach Step 3, then attach them. In flight, refresh by adding new creatives into the existing ad group and archiving the tired ones — never by moving assets between groups. If a creative needs to live in two ad groups, duplicate it under a unique name rather than moving it.

And do not stop a campaign that has momentum to make a creative change. If the change genuinely requires a different structure, let the current flight finish and build the next one properly.

Source: Demandbase Help Center (duplication guidance); TP internal — 30 Apr 2026 Kate Varvedo session, recommendation not to stop campaigns with good momentum.

Pre-flight checklist

  • Inventory Planner run and reach cleared 80%, ideally 90%+
  • SFDC campaign code exists and the campaign name matches it
  • [US] prefix on the campaign, account list, person list and keyword set
  • Geo: United States and Canada ticked individually; Mexico unchecked
  • Journey = US Journey — confirmed on screen, not assumed
  • Buying groups match the motion, and the person list belongs to this campaign
  • No funnel bucket holding 0 accounts has ad groups attached
  • Two ad groups per funnel — one Standard, one User retargeting — four creatives each
  • Five or more display sizes uploaded; 300×250 and 320×50 present if In-App is on
  • Landing pages live, carrying the Demandbase pixel, the Marketo Munchkin and a unique UTM tied to a smart campaign
  • Bid ladder set — standard / more / most across Aware, Engaged, Pipeline
  • Watchers added — vertical owner and EVP

Note: the UTM item is gated on the Umbraco → Marketo hidden-field wiring. Until that is live, click-through attribution will not reconcile end to end — plan the campaign anyway, but do not promise the attribution.
