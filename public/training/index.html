Who owns this

Demandbase Operations owns the platform layer and the training on it — system notifications and monitoring, the intake request queue, orchestration paths, audience builds and pushes to LinkedIn, and the Marketo side of smart campaign setup.

The mandate is to be in the room. On the ABM calls, fielding the questions as they come up, finding what is broken and fixing what can be fixed from the platform side. Not a ticket queue you file into and wait — someone who is already on the call when the question is asked.

What it does not cover is running your campaign. The person who builds a campaign is the person who has to read it afterwards, so the build stays with the marketer. Operations sets up the surface, pushes the audience, and tells you plainly what the platform will and will not do.

How to ask for a session

Use the intake form and put the question you cannot answer in the request. That is what shapes the hour. "Training on Demandbase" gets a generic walkthrough; "I cannot tell which ad group drove the MQAs on Cyber Defense" gets an hour that changes something.

New to the team, or new to the stack? Say so in the request rather than working it out alone. A walkthrough is a normal ask and it is faster than three weeks of guessing. The sales-side sessions run separately with the Demandbase CSM — two or three focused hours rather than one long one.

Never guess at a field definition. If what a field means is unclear, ask rather than assume — a wrong definition repeated in a deck is far more expensive than a question asked in a call.

And if the platform genuinely cannot answer it, say so out loud. Some questions have no surface today: advertising click data, person-level coverage below Tier 3, anonymous account influence with no form fill. Those are gaps in the stack, not gaps in you. Naming them is how they get on a roadmap.
