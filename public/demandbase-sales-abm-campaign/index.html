Every prompt below starts with your full name and "US accounts only." Fill in the braces. Run the find prompt first, then the brief prompt in the same thread so it builds on the answer.
1:1

1:1 for the accounts that matter most

Find the accounts
I'm {your full name}, a Business Developer covering {vertical}. From my US accounts only, find the 5 to 10 accounts I should consider for 1:1 campaigns. Prioritize high Pipeline Predict or trending intent on {topics}, with few engaged people or no open opportunity. For each, show journey stage, Pipeline Predict score, top 3 intent topics with dates, people engaged in the last 90 days, and known decision makers by role. Then flag the buying-group roles where we have no engagement.

You get: a ranked short list with the gaps marked. Pick the ones worth the spend.

Write the brief
For {company}, write a one-page 1:1 campaign brief for my ABM manager. Include: why now (intent topics and dates, journey stage, recent engagement); the buying group to reach, US-based only, with names, titles and roles; what Salesforce shows (open or past opportunities, current TP services); the three themes I'm hearing in my conversations: {your notes}; the outcome I want: {a meeting with role by date}; and what I'll do alongside the ads (calls, emails, events). Under 300 words. Do not write ad copy.

You get: a brief your ABM manager can plan from without a meeting.

1:few

1:few for clusters that are stuck

Find the clusters
I'm {your full name}. From my US accounts only, find accounts at the aware or engaged stage that haven't changed stage in 90 days. Group them into clusters of 25 to 50 that share an intent theme or line of business, such as {CX, trust and safety, AI operations}. For each cluster, give the shared theme, the account count, the top intent topics and the roles most engaged.

You get: two or three clusters with a reason each one hangs together.

Write the brief
Turn the {cluster name} cluster into a 1:few campaign brief for my ABM manager: the theme, the account list, why these accounts belong together, the proof points TP should lead with based on what they're researching, the roles to reach, the outcome I want, and accounts to exclude (open opportunities, clients I already cover, competitors). Under 300 words. Do not write ad copy.
1:many

1:many for the quiet accounts

Find the quiet accounts and the trigger
I'm {your full name}. Find my US accounts at the target or aware stage with little or no engagement in the last 90 days. Recommend which ones should go into a 1:many awareness campaign for one to two quarters, grouped by vertical. Tell me which engagement signals should hand an account back to me or an SDR, and what my next step is for the accounts left out.

You get: an air-cover list and the signal that tells you when to pick the phone back up.

Acceleration

Acceleration for the stuck deals

Find the deals and write the brief
I'm {your full name}. Show my open opportunities on US accounts that have been in the same stage for more than {60} days. For each: stage, days in stage, buying-group contacts engaged in the last 30 days, intent topics and missing roles. Then write a short acceleration brief for my ABM manager: which deals, which people to reach, the angle based on what they're researching, and the meeting I want next.
After launch

The weekly check

Every Monday once it's live
For the accounts in {campaign or list name}, show which ones moved journey stage, gained engagement or showed new intent since {launch date}. Rank the ones I should call this week and tell me why.
Ad engagement is counted by company, not by person. The people it lists are contacts at engaged accounts, not confirmed clickers. Treat them as warm accounts to work, not as people who clicked.
Checklist

What a good request includes

  • The accounts and the motion: 1:1, 1:few, 1:many or acceleration.
  • Why now: the intent topics and dates, the journey stage, the engagement.
  • The people: the roles you need to reach, and the ones you already talk to.
  • Your field insight: what you're hearing that the data can't show.
  • The outcome and the date you need it by, plus the accounts to exclude.
  • Your plan alongside the ads, so the touches line up.
Plan ahead. A LinkedIn audience needs about a week to match before ads can run, and new intent keywords take a few days to collect signals. Get your brief to your ABM manager early. These are long prompts, so send one well-built prompt instead of five quick ones, and check five rows against the account page before you act on an answer.
