Before you start

Two things that will save you a week

You probably cannot build the audience yourself.

Audience creation is admin-gated in Demandbase. Ask Josh or Sharath to build it, then point your ad set at it. Do not wait around expecting to self-serve.

Everything happens in the US Workspace.

Audiences are workspace-scoped, so this is not the Global-only import path. Moving one between workspaces is permanent and only flows up. Build it in US and leave it there.

Say this out loud before you launch: targeting works today, measurement does not. The LinkedIn Insight Tag is missing from the 2026 Marketo landing pages but firing on the thank-you page — so conversions will read zero while impressions, clicks and CTR report normally.

That is a tracking gap, not an audience failure. If you do not flag it up front, it gets reported as a broken integration and everyone chases the wrong thing.

The play

Push an audience to LinkedIn

Demandbase builds and pushes it. LinkedIn receives it as a matched audience. Two systems, two vocabularies, and the words do not line up — which is exactly where people get stuck.

The thing that trips everyone. What pushes to LinkedIn is an Orchestration Audience — not a Person List. Different objects, and only the Audience has a Destinations tab. Build a list expecting a push button and you will not find one.

Side one

In Demandbase

Member Type is locked at creation and cannot be changed later. Pick it first.

01

Create the audience

Orchestration > Audiences then Create New. Name it and set Member Type to Person. This decides which destinations appear later.

02

Point the selector at a real list

Use Member of List IN … against a governed list so the audience inherits our tiering logic. Building criteria from scratch works, but it drifts from everything else.

03

Check Member Preview before you go further

Confirm actual people are resolving, not just a count. A person audience only sends the email addresses it holds — if they did not resolve, the audience is thin and will never clear LinkedIn's floor.

04

Add LinkedIn as a destination

Destinations tab, then +, then LinkedIn. Choose Create New, Create New And Replace, or Use Existing, and pick the ad account. Confirm it is the US account.

05

Schedule and activate

Nothing moves until you do this. Person audiences send email addresses, account audiences send domains.

Side two

In LinkedIn

Campaign Manager renamed its hierarchy in October 2025. What we used to call a campaign is now an ad set — this catches nearly everyone.

06

Find it

Plan > Audiences then the Matched tab. Source column reads Third Party. Demandbase shows up as the audience name, not as the source value.

07

Attach it to the ad set

In the Audience section switch to Third Party Audiences, then Third Party Contact for people or Third Party Company for accounts. It also works as an exclusion list — handy for suppressing existing clients.

The numbers

What decides whether it actually delivers

300

Minimum to serve

LinkedIn's rule, not ours. Demandbase will happily push 50 people and the error only shows up in Campaign Manager.

10,000+

Emails to push in

LinkedIn's own guidance to reliably land 300 verified matches. Multiple lists can be combined in one ad set.

24–48h

First sync

Then nightly, automatic. It is not a static dump — it stays aligned to the selector.

The silent killer: the LinkedIn access token expires after one year. Nobody owns a re-auth calendar, so the sync just stops and the audience quietly goes stale while still reading Ready.

Why this matters

A matched audience is built from people, not companies

That one fact drives the entire tiering argument. You can have a beautiful account list and still fail to deliver, because LinkedIn is counting contacts — and when you slice them by persona and funnel stage, thin accounts disappear.

What went wrong

The seniority bucket trap

A campaign ran with a wide seniority range instead of being confined to the pushed T4/T5 matched list. LinkedIn's own Owner, Founder and Partner buckets pulled clicks from independent consultants and self-employed titles — people in none of our buying groups. We were paying for traffic outside the named account universe.

Our buying groups are C-Suite, VP, Head and Director across CX Leaders, LOB Leaders and Vertical Specialist Leaders, plus Procurement & Sourcing and Finance & Accounting. Independent consultant is not one of them.

The fix. Scale the audience down to the pushed list and let the buying groups do the targeting. Align titles to our persona set, never LinkedIn's seniority buckets.

Sequencing. If the scope moves to T2 through T5, the push audience has to be rebuilt at that scope before the campaign changes. Do it the other way round and you build it twice.

Expectation setting

A low match rate is not a broken integration

Company lists match better than contact lists. Corporate email addresses often do not match a LinkedIn profile keyed to a personal address, so a lower contact match rate is normal.

Do not quote a target match rate from memory. Read the LinkedIn 7-Day Match Rate that Demandbase surfaces and work from that.
