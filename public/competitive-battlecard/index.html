TTEC Holdings (NASDAQ: TTEC) is a CX/BPO company in structural decline. Revenue has fallen 18% from its FY2023 peak of $2.46B to guided FY2026 of $2.03B, with three consecutive years of GAAP net losses totaling over $495M. The company carries $892M in debt against a market cap of ~$104M — a ratio of 8.6x. A critical interest rate step-up from SOFR+3% to SOFR+6% takes effect October 1, 2026, and the facility matures November 2027. Founder/CEO Ken Tuchman attempted a take-private at $6.85/share in September 2024 but withdrew in July 2025 because external financing was unobtainable — the stock now trades at ~$2.14. The company has taken $438M in goodwill impairments across two consecutive years, has lost three senior leaders in 18 months, and suspended 401(k) matching. Their AI story consists of five separately branded point tools with no unified platform architecture. For enterprise CX buyers entering 2026 contract cycles, TTEC's financial fragility is a material risk.
