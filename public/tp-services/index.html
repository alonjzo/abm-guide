TP.ai Operate is not a product like the others

Operate is the delivery engine — how TP builds and runs the operation behind any service. Hiring, learning, workforce efficiency, performance management, insights. Its descriptor is “AI-powered solution for highly efficient ops.”

You will recognize it on the CX pages without the name attached: Set Up → Scale → Stabilize → Optimize is Operate, described in client language. That is why it usually shows up attached to another product rather than sold on its own.

Brand rule — read before you build anything. The five Operate capability lockups (Hiring, Learning, Workforce Efficiency, Performance Management, Insights) must never appear on websites, external campaigns, events, printed materials or any public-facing communication. Client presentations only, and only after Operate has been introduced as the primary solution. The main signature — TP.ai Operate plus its descriptor — is fine anywhere.

Buyer hears: "You run my pipeline."

Product package

TP.ai Growth

The AI suite that powers it — lead sourcing, enrichment, outreach automation, sales intelligence, coaching.

Naming and structure issues

Observed on the live site

Four more for the brand/web list, on top of the three in the first decoder.

Delivery models live at CX-services URLs

All three sit under /services/cx-services/ despite being their own nav column. That means GA4 landing-page and content-group reporting will roll them into CX Services unless someone has manually carved them out. Worth checking before the next channel readout — it inflates the CX Services content group and hides delivery-model interest entirely.

Two specialized brands have no metrics; two have unusually strong ones

TLScontact and the delivery-model pages publish no numbers at all, while PSG posts 4× recruiter output and ~50% cost savings. That asymmetry reads as inconsistency rather than modesty. If the sovereign and delivery businesses have defensible numbers, they belong on the page; if they can't be published, a qualitative proof point still beats a blank.

The brand house isn't explained anywhere

Health Advocate, LanguageLine, PSG and TLScontact appear as service names with the parent brand buried in body copy. A prospect can't tell these are acquired companies with decades of independent equity — which is exactly the thing that makes them credible. One line per page ("a TP company since —") would do a lot of work.

"Interpretation" in the nav, "Translation and Interpretation" on the page

Minor, but the nav label drops half the offering, and the page's primary CTA sends visitors to LanguageLine.com — off tp.com. Any paid or organic spend landing there exits the measured estate. Worth a UTM and cross-domain tracking check.
